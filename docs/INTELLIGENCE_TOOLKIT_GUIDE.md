# ZRC Intelligence Toolkit — User Guide

## Purpose
ZRC Intelligence is a decision-support workflow, not a collection of isolated dashboards. Start with a signal, test geopolitical and macro transmission, examine financial implications, and finish with a documented decision hypothesis.

> Informational and analytical only; not personalised investment, legal, tax or financial advice.

## Recommended workflow
**Detect → Frame → Stress-test → Translate → Decide → Monitor**

### 1. Detect — Observatory
Start in the **Geopolitical Observatory**. Identify a material signal: conflict escalation, sanctions, trade restrictions, energy disruption, elections, central-bank communication or a macro release.

Ask: What changed? Is it new or an extension of a trend? Which countries, sectors, commodities, currencies or supply chains are exposed? What would make it economically material?

**Output:** one-sentence risk hypothesis.

### 2. Frame — GeoRisk Index
Use the **ZRC GeoRisk Index** to establish the broader risk regime. Compare the current level, direction, persistence and whether multiple risk channels are moving together. The index is context, not a trading signal.

**Output:** baseline geopolitical regime.

### 3. Stress-test — GeoRisk Dashboard
The **GeoRisk Dashboard** is the core scenario tool.

1. Define the scenario before moving controls.
2. Set the relevant risk drivers with the scenario sliders.
3. Change one major assumption at a time first so sensitivity remains visible.
4. Observe estimated 12-month effects on the available asset variables.
5. Build three cases: **Base**, **Adverse**, and **Relief**.
6. Record which assumptions create the largest change in outputs.
7. Treat results as scenario estimates, not point forecasts.

#### Worked example — Strait of Hormuz disruption
**Hypothesis:** sustained disruption raises the geopolitical risk premium and creates an energy-led inflation shock.

Test severity and persistence, energy/supply-chain transmission, and differences between base/adverse/relief cases. Then ask whether inflation, growth, risk premia or several channels dominate; which outputs are most sensitive; whether effects are temporary or persistent; and what evidence would invalidate the scenario.

**Output:** scenario matrix with assumptions, transmission channels, affected variables and invalidation triggers.

### 4. Challenge — GeoRisk Predictive ML
Use **GeoRisk Predictive ML** after framing the scenario. Use its AI/NLP forecasting and correlation views to challenge the manual case: does it support the direction, reveal missed relationships, or disagree with the analyst?

A disagreement is a reason to investigate—not automatically to choose either the model or the analyst.

**Output:** model-supported or model-challenged hypothesis.

### 5. Translate policy — Macro Pulse
Use **Macro Pulse** when the scenario affects inflation, rates, liquidity or currencies. Examine central-bank divergence, policy tone, rate-cycle direction and whether the shock changes the expected reaction function.

**Output:** policy-transmission view.

### 6. Translate to the company — Financial Intelligence System
For a company or SME, use **FIS** to translate the scenario into revenue sensitivity, input costs/margins, working capital, financing costs, liquidity, 13-week cash flow and principal risks.

Use the chain:
**Event → economic variable → company exposure → cash-flow effect → financing/strategic implication.**

**Output:** corporate impact case.

### 7. Asset context — Real Estate Visor
For Spanish real estate, use **Real Estate Visor** for cadastral geolocation and available risk/planning layers. Combine asset-level information with the macro scenario rather than treating property data in isolation.

**Output:** asset context + macro overlay.

## 10-minute Intelligence routine
- **0–2 min:** Observatory — identify one consequential signal.
- **2–3 min:** GeoRisk Index — establish the risk regime.
- **3–6 min:** GeoRisk Dashboard — base/adverse/relief.
- **6–8 min:** GeoRisk ML or Macro Pulse — challenge the case and inspect policy transmission.
- **8–10 min:** write the decision note.

Use five lines:
1. **Signal:** What changed?
2. **Transmission:** Through which economic channels?
3. **Scenario:** Base / adverse / relief.
4. **Exposure:** Which assets, sectors or corporate variables are affected?
5. **Trigger:** What would confirm or invalidate the view?

## Analyst discipline
- Separate **facts, assumptions, model outputs and judgement**.
- Never interpret model estimates as certainties.
- Change assumptions systematically.
- State the time horizon.
- Record source and date of material inputs.
- Define an invalidation trigger for every material scenario.
- Re-run when the event, policy response or market regime changes.
- Combine tools when the question crosses geopolitical, macro and financial channels.

## Use by audience
**Students:** connect macroeconomic models to live events; formulate the transmission mechanism, then test scenarios. The objective is analytical reasoning, not investment recommendations.

**Investors and analysts:** structure scenarios, expose assumptions and identify transmission channels before security-specific due diligence.

**CEOs and CFOs:** translate external shocks into prices, demand, FX, rates, working capital, liquidity and financing capacity.

**Advisors:** create a consistent evidence chain from external event to strategic or corporate-finance implication.

## Quality standard
A strong ZRC Intelligence analysis is reproducible and answers:

**What happened? → Why does it matter? → Through what mechanism? → Under which assumptions? → What is exposed? → What would change our view?**
